📖 What is convolution?

Convolution is how computers “look” at images. A tiny grid of numbers called a kernel (or filter) slides across the picture one pixel at a time. At every stop, each kernel number is multiplied by the pixel underneath it, and all those products are added up. That single sum becomes one pixel of a brand-new image called the feature map. Change the numbers and you change what the filter notices: it can blur, sharpen, or light up edges. This one simple operation is the building block of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)!

ℹ️ psst… a tiny secret for the math nerds

Strictly speaking, true convolution flips the kernel 180° before sliding it. What this app (and almost every deep-learning library) computes is cross-correlation, with no flip. For symmetric kernels like blur, both give the same answer. Formula used here: out(x, y) = Σi Σj K(i, j) · I(x·s + i − p, y·s + j − p) where s = stride and p = padding.
